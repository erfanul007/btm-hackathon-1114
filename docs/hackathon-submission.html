Speaking script

At Level 1, we impersonated a doctor and obtained patient information. The request sounded like a normal care workflow, so the assistant accepted the claimed role without verifying who was asking. That is the core weakness: sounding authorized became a substitute for being authorized. The practical fix is verified identity and record access before any patient lookup.

Evidence and qualifications — reference only

30 seconds. At Level 1 the protection is primarily an instruction to keep data private. We used doctor impersonation to make disclosure sound like legitimate care. The team confirms this pass; an exact level-tagged doctor-demo turn or model is not identified in the imported files, so we do not invent either. Speaking script

At Level 2, an output filter added protection. We asked for contact fields encoded inside SQL, received encoded text, and decoded it locally. In a historical DeepSeek experiment, four of twenty hex exports contained recoverable patient contacts, even though the original detector marked them safe. The lesson is simple: encoding changes how the data looks, not whether it is sensitive.

Evidence and qualifications — reference only

35 seconds. For Level 2, we moved the transformation into SQL, obtained the encoded values, then decoded locally. The team confirms the Level 2 pass. Four historical DeepSeek hex exports in 20 runs corroborate the mechanism. Their exact level was not saved; this is a historical experiment rather than a model-specific Level 2 pass rate. Turn IDs: e310b103f6a9460b9127c4fbe2cedcc3; ff258d8786ef4f039fd5b8a2bd0bb466; 958ad938b8e64905bfad93440ed41963; b55a9e95ed3d403ea1dbe0fab33c277c. Encoding cannot recover an SSN that was redacted before the model saw it. Our decode-aware scanner exposed this measurement gap.

06 / WHAT WE LEARNED

Verify the caller.
Constrain the tool.
Inspect what leaves.

01 · IDENTITY

Verify the role

A doctor persona should not unlock a patient record.

02 · ACCESS

Limit fields and records

Enforce permissions before data reaches the model.

03 · OUTPUT

Decode before scanning

Inspect encoded values as well as visible text.

Delivered: reproducible attack evidence + a decode-aware detector prototype.
Level 1 passed · Level 2 passed · Level 3 / Claude inconclusive

Speaking script

Our conclusion is three controls: verify the caller, constrain what each tool can access, and inspect what leaves—including encoded values. We delivered preserved attack evidence and a decode-aware detector prototype. Levels 1 and 2 passed; a sensitive-data break on Claude Level 3 remains unproven.

Evidence and qualifications — reference only

25 seconds. Our conclusion is that prompts and plaintext filters are incomplete security boundaries. The strongest control is to verify the caller and constrain the data available to every tool request. Our implemented defense.py normalizes formatting and decodes base64/hex before scanning. It is a detector prototype, not a deployed blocking gateway. The historical 96% precision and 79% recall claims were not independently reproducible from the supplied labels, so they are omitted. No live tests were rerun for this deck. Historical requests omit level attribution. We do not claim a full 413-patient dump, recovered SSNs, delivered external exfiltration or a current Claude Level 3 breach. Total planned speaking time: 180 seconds.
